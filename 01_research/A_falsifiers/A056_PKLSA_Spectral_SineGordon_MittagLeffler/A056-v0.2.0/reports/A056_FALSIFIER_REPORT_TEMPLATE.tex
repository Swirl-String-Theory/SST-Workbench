\section{Objective}
This falsifier asks whether an independently generated phase observable $\varphi(s,t)$ on a perturbed vortex filament requires (i) an effective Sine--Gordon restoring law and/or (ii) Mittag--Leffler fractional-memory relaxation.  The test is adversarial: the target models compete against preregistered alternatives, and discovery cannot certify itself.

\section{Source contract}
A real input case contains monotone grids $t_i$ and $s_j$, a finite array $\varphi_{ij}=\varphi(t_i,s_j)$, and optional relaxation amplitude $R(t)$.  Metadata freeze the phase definition, perturbation, solver/provider version, upstream geometry SHA-256, carrier SHA-256, and independent source group.  Static XYZ geometry alone is inadmissible.

\section{Exact derivatives tested by code}
For uniform spacings $\Delta t$ and $\Delta s$, the Python and C++ kernels compute
\begin{align}
\varphi_{tt}(t_i,s_j)&\approx\frac{\varphi_{i+1,j}-2\varphi_{i,j}+\varphi_{i-1,j}}{(\Delta t)^2},\\
\varphi_{ss}(t_i,s_j)&\approx\frac{\varphi_{i,j+1}-2\varphi_{i,j}+\varphi_{i,j-1}}{(\Delta s)^2}.
\end{align}
Periodic indexing is used only when the provider declares a periodic spatial boundary.

\section{Phase-model competition}
The discovery fit compares
\begin{align}
H_W &: \varphi_{tt}=a\varphi_{ss},\\
H_{KG} &: \varphi_{tt}=a\varphi_{ss}-b\varphi,\\
H_D &: \varphi_{tt}=a\varphi_{ss}-b\varphi-c\varphi^3,\\
H_{SG} &: \varphi_{tt}=a\varphi_{ss}-b\sin\varphi.
\end{align}
The first 65\% of time samples define discovery.  Parameters are obtained by least squares.  The Bayesian information criterion is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BIC}=n\ln(\mathrm{RSS}/n)+k\ln n.
\end{equation}
Sine--Gordon discovery requires positive $a,b$, acceptable design conditioning, and $\Delta\mathrm{BIC}_{SG}\ge10$.  The remaining 35\% of time samples are never used to fit the coefficients; confirmation requires its normalized RMSE to beat the best competitor by the frozen ratio threshold.

\section{Spectral qualification}
The centered phase matrix $X$ is decomposed by singular-value decomposition,
\begin{equation}
X=U\Sigma V^{\mathsf T}.
\end{equation}
The test records leading modal energy, orthogonality residual, numerical rank, and the principal-subspace overlap between discovery and confirmation time blocks.  This is a numerical qualification layer, not a claim that POD modes are quantum states.

\section{Relaxation-model competition}
The relaxation models are
\begin{align}
R_{\exp}(t)&=e^{-t/\tau},\\
R_{\mathrm{stretch}}(t)&=e^{-(t/\tau)^\beta},\\
R_{2\exp}(t)&=w e^{-t/\tau_1}+(1-w)e^{-t/\tau_2},\\
R_{ML}(t)&=E_{\alpha,1}\!\left[-(t/\tau)^\alpha\right],
\end{align}
where
\begin{equation}
E_{\alpha,\beta}(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^k}{\Gamma(\alpha k+\beta)}.
\end{equation}
The identity
\begin{equation}
E_{1,1}(-t/\tau)=e^{-t/\tau}
\end{equation}
is an explicit null limit.  Therefore fractional-memory support additionally requires $0.48<\alpha<0.97$.

\section{Numerical certification}
A confirmed phase case is recomputed after deterministic twofold coarsening in both $t$ and $s$.  For the Sine--Gordon coefficient vector $\mathbf c=(a,b)$ the reported convergence diagnostic is
\begin{equation}
\delta_c=\frac{\|\mathbf c_{\rm fine}-\mathbf c_{\rm coarse}\|_2}{\max(\|\mathbf c_{\rm fine}\|_2,10^{-30})}.
\end{equation}
A confirmed memory case is independently re-fit after twofold time decimation and must retain its fitted fractional order and timescale within frozen tolerances,
\begin{equation}
\delta_\alpha=|\alpha_{\rm fine}-\alpha_{\rm coarse}|,
\qquad
\delta_\tau=\frac{|\tau_{\rm fine}-\tau_{\rm coarse}|}{\max(|\tau_{\rm fine}|,10^{-30})}.
\end{equation}
This candidate-specific split prevents a memory-only case from being rejected because an irrelevant Sine--Gordon coefficient is resolution-sensitive.  Production profiles also require the corresponding C++/OpenMP kernel to agree with the CPU/Python reference within the preregistered parity tolerance.  SYCL is screening-only.
