\section{Research question and objective}
\subsection{Single falsifiable research question}
After excluding the automatic complex-conjugate partner of a real projected Jacobian, does any anonymous three-component vortex compound contain two \emph{distinct positive-temporal-frequency} Kelvin-dominated modes with opposite signed spatial propagation, matched in frequency under the frozen threshold, and recurring in at least two circulation sectors?

\subsection{Scientific objective}
The objective is to replace an algebraically non-discriminating frequency-sign test by a spatial propagation observable. The test reconstructs complex eigenfields in physical space, measures their signed arclength-Fourier content, and then asks whether opposite directions coexist as distinct positive-frequency branches. Linear mode evidence is kept separate from spatial convergence, relative-periodic-orbit (RPO) recurrence, and conditional Floquet evidence.

% SST-REPORT-SECTION:EQUATIONS
\section{Exact mathematical model and observables}
For component $c$, the reconstructed complex eigenfield is projected on a deterministic periodic Bishop parallel-transport transverse frame with the closed-loop holonomy distributed continuously over arclength to obtain complex scalar series $a_{n,c}(s)$ and $a_{b,c}(s)$. For arclength phase $\theta_c(s)\in[0,2\pi)$ and harmonic $k$,
\begin{equation}
P_{j,k}^{\pm}=\sum_c\left(|\widehat a_{n,c}(\pm k)|^2+|\widehat a_{b,c}(\pm k)|^2\right),
\label{eq:E1}
\end{equation}
with Fourier coefficients computed directly at the arclength phases. Directional purity is
\begin{equation}
\chi_{j,k}=\frac{P_{j,k}^{+}-P_{j,k}^{-}}{P_{j,k}^{+}+P_{j,k}^{-}},
\label{eq:E2}
\end{equation}
and harmonic participation is
\begin{equation}
\eta_{j,k}=\frac{P_{j,k}^{+}+P_{j,k}^{-}}{P_{j,\perp}}.
\label{eq:E3}
\end{equation}
Two distinct positive-frequency modes of opposite spatial direction at the same $k$ are frequency matched by
\begin{equation}
A_\omega=\frac{|\omega_1-\omega_2|}{(\omega_1+\omega_2)/2}.
\label{eq:E4}
\end{equation}
Because the upstream projected basis is orthonormal and carries frozen family labels, the Kelvin-family coefficient fraction is
\begin{equation}
\zeta_j=\frac{\sum_{i\in\mathcal K}|v_{ij}|^2}{\sum_i|v_{ij}|^2}.
\label{eq:E6}
\end{equation}
The conjugate-invariant physical direction is equivalently
\begin{equation}
d_{j,k}=\operatorname{sgn}(\omega_j\chi_{j,k}).
\label{eq:E5}
\end{equation}
Under conjugation, $\omega\mapsto-\omega$ and $\chi\mapsto-\chi$, so $d$ is unchanged. The implementation uses only the $\omega>0$ representative; an automatic conjugate partner therefore cannot create the opposite direction.
