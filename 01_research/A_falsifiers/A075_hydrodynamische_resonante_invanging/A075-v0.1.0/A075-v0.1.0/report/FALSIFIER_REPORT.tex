\section{Identification and status}
Catalog A075; family \texttt{hydrodynamische\_resonante\_invanging}; version v0.1.0. The frozen framework is the unmodified shared v1.0.10 runtime, loaded through \texttt{framework\_bootstrap.py}. Results are a \emph{regularized filament-model screening}, not a qualified full three-dimensional (3D) Euler solution or photon model.
% SST-REPORT-SECTION:QUESTION
\section{Question and objective}
Can two or more completely coupled, closed vorticity filaments sustain a lasting excess of incoming-excitation energy in a receiver without damping, imposed speed matching, or a preferred azimuthal mode? This first release checks the necessary numerical conservation and far-separation controls and explicitly leaves the central energy-capture claim unresolved.
% SST-REPORT-SECTION:HYPOTHESES
\section{Competing hypotheses}
$H_0$: the short-run change of the receiver self-Hamiltonian is ordinary conservative energy redistribution; a capture fraction is not defined without a resolved local-energy flux and post-encounter window. $H_1$: a bona fide finite-energy incoming perturbation exhibits reproducible excess local energy at late time with spatial and temporal convergence and a qualified full-fluid mechanism provider. A short-run numerical PASS cannot establish $H_1$.
% SST-REPORT-SECTION:ASSUMPTIONS
\section{Assumptions and limitations}
Constant-density, incompressible inviscid fluid background; two backreacting closed, co-axial centerlines; smoothing radius $a>0$; no reconnection, external forcing, imposed speed matching, damping or imposed azimuthal perturbation. Regularized filament kinematics is not the complete 3D Euler partial differential equation with pressure-Poisson closure. Effective finite-core structure is prescribed rather than resolved. No atomic material response, Maxwell coupling or optical absorption is modeled.

\section{Equations and measured observables}
\begin{align}
\dot{\mathbf x}_i&=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_j\Gamma_j\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol\ell_j\times(\mathbf x_i-\mathbf m_j)}{(|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf m_j|^2+a^2)^{3/2}},\\
H_a&=\frac{\rhoF}{8\pi}\sum_{ij}\frac{\Gamma_i\Gamma_j\,\Delta\boldsymbol\ell_i\cdot\Delta\boldsymbol\ell_j}{\sqrt{|\mathbf m_i-\mathbf m_j|^2+a^2}},\\
H_a&=H_{RR}+H_{LL}+H_{RL},\\
\epsilon_H&=\max_t\frac{|H_a(t)-H_a(0)|}{\max(|H_a(0)|,\epsilon_0)}.
\end{align}
Only $H_{RR}(t)-H_{RR}(0)$ is recorded as a \emph{receiver self-energy proxy}; it is not the trapped incoming energy. A qualified $f_{ret}$ additionally requires a physical control-volume energy-flux calculation.

% SST-REPORT-SECTION:ALGORITHM
\section{Algorithm: equation-to-code mapping}
Build two closed rings with $N=24$ or 32 segments, smoothing radius $a=0.22$ in normalized SI-compatible units, $\Gamma_R=\Gamma_L=1\,\mathrm{m^2s^{-1}}$, $\rhoF=1\,\mathrm{kgm^{-3}}$. Integrate both centerlines with explicit fourth-order Runge--Kutta to a fixed final time $T=1\,\mathrm s$ at $\Delta t=0.05$ and $0.025\,\mathrm s$. Test $H_a$ and impulse, a far-separation control, and independent spatial/temporal refinement. No late-time capture status is assigned.

% SST-REPORT-SECTION:NUMERICS
\section{Numerical convergence strategy}
Fixed absolute end time across all resolution ladders; separate $\Delta t$ and $N$ controls; record $H_{RR}$, $H_{LL}$ and $H_{RL}$, total energy and Kelvin impulse. Production needs finite-core width convergence, controlled arclength remeshing, independent initial-state holdouts and sufficiently long observation windows. A short-run PASS is explicitly diagnostic only.
