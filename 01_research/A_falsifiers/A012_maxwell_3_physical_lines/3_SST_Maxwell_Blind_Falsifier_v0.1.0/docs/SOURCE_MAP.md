# Historical source map

Primary historical source: J. Clerk Maxwell, **On Physical Lines of Force**, Philosophical Magazine, Parts I–IV (1861–1862).

- Part I, journal pp. 453–458: stress with one preferred axis; least axial pressure interpreted as tension; molecular-vortex mechanism; pressure differences scaling with density and velocity squared.
- Part I, journal pp. 455–457: for geometrically similar vortex systems Maxwell explicitly obtains pressure scaling proportional to density and the square of velocity, with no dependence on linear size. His numerical coefficient for a particular uniform circular-vortex model is a historical benchmark, not a preregistered SST target.
- Part II/III transition, journal p. 479: Maxwell introduces the **reduced momentum** of a machine at a driving point and states that the force due to changing motion is obtained by differentiating this reduced momentum in time; he uses this as the mechanical analogue of the electrotonic state and electromotive force.
- Part III, journal pp. 491–495: electric displacement is treated as elastic yielding; its variation constitutes a current. This motivates an optional structural-storage test, not a claim that the incompressible SST substrate is compressible.
- Summary around journal pp. 485–486: Maxwell notes that the force effect can be independent of vortex diameter while angular momentum is not. This motivates the angular-momentum cancellation guard.
